Size is median_n_strikes_clean — surviving strikes per fitted expiry, a liquidity proxy. This database has no volume, ADV or market-cap column and no sector table, so the spec's "colour by sector, size by liquidity" has no source; colour is an optional metric on a diverging ramp instead.
IV level ?
Today's distribution of ATM IV z across the universe, median marked. If the median ticker sits at +0.4σ, a ticker at +2.0σ is riding a universe-wide move rather than standing out on its own — same number, different trade. Nothing else on the page answers "is this ticker unusual, or is everything unusual". The LEVEL rather than the skew, because the skew is already the subject of the two panels beside it.
Breadth ? …
A line rather than a second histogram: the panel beside it is already a distribution, and repeating that form would add a shape rather than a fact. This one says whether TODAY'S distribution is itself unusual, which a histogram of today cannot. Both tails, because breadth one way is not the complement of the other — 8% rich with 6% cheap is a dispersed universe, 8% rich with 0% cheap is a directional one. The risk reversal rather than the skew, so this is wing asymmetry as a raw vol difference rather than the scatter's slope measured again in different units.
Skew × return ? …
The adverse-selection check at universe level: are the rich-skew names the ones that fell? A tight cluster with a flat y-axis is a different universe from a downward slope. Coloured by term state — blue contango, pink backwardation — and NOT by sector: this database has no sector table, nor a volume, ADV or market-cap column. Term state is the regime filter and the third axis of the same question, so it adds a dimension rather than decorating one. The y axis follows the page tenor. Read it as what it is: It runs through the PRIOR session's close, so it does not contain today — comparing it against a live spot to back out a starting price gives the wrong answer, because the return does not end where the spot is. The spot panel below is the one that moves intraday.
Contango ? % of universe · 20 sessions …
Two lines and a 50% rule rather than stacked pairs: contango and backwardation sum to the covered universe, so a stack would spend half the panel drawing the complement of the other half. A term ratio is near/far, so below 1 is contango. The two pairs are FIXED at 7/30 and 30/90 and do not follow the page tenor — the subject is the shape of the curve, and a shape needs two points on it. The front pair moving while the body holds is an event calendar; both moving together is a vol shock.
Tape ? % up · day so far open pending …
The only panel here that moves through the session. Every other metric on this row is either a vol reading at the selected bucket or — in the scatter's case — a settled daily-close series that will not change until tonight. Two anchors: against last night's close is the gap plus the day, against today's open is the day alone. 60% above the close with 45% above the open is a universe that gapped up and has been sold since, which one line cannot say. The open comes from underlying_ohlc's daily bar, not the 0935 snapshot — that bucket is minutes into the session and already carries whatever moved in them. The cost is that the daily bar is not written until after the close, so the open-anchored line is absent for the whole live session and says "open pending" rather than substituting a number that is not the open. SPY/QQQ/IWM ride along as stepped lines at 100% or 0% — above or below their own prior close — and are NOT in the breadth count: an index ETF is a weighted basket of the names being counted, so including it would double-count its constituents. Read against the breadth line on the same axis, that IS the panel's question: SPY pinned at 100 with breadth at 40% is a narrow tape. They sat on a second y-axis as percent moves until the axis itself proved more expensive than the precision — the move is in the tooltip, which is where an exact number belongs anyway.
Premium ? …
What share of the universe is being paid to sell vol at the page tenor. The LEVEL is the reading here, not the tail: 80% is a universe where short vol is the default and the edge is in picking; 45% is one where it is a position. No z on this line — a positive premium is a fact about the number itself, not about where the number sits in its own history, and scoring it would have turned "is it paid" into "is it paid more than usual", which the breadth panel already asks of another metric.

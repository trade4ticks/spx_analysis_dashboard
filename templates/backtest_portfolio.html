Portfolio profiles
A profile holds the strategies, their qty, capital and filters, and the date-range mode — not the trades. Re-save a strategy on the other page and the next load picks up the new file.
Strategies
Saved on the OO/Mesosim Backtest page. Loading re-uses that page's parse, market join and columns, so a strategy reads the same here as it does there.
Date range
Union uses everything, and its early stretch has fewer strategies in it. Intersection compares like with like and throws the rest away. Either way a trade belongs to the window it was OPENED in.
Allocation
qty scales P/L linearly. Capital seeds from the strategy's saved value and is this page's until a profile saves it; portfolio capital is the sum.

* Sharpe is the old app's: P/L summed by close date, mean/stdev times the square root of 252, with days that had no close not zero-filled. It ranks these rows against each other and is not comparable with a published Sharpe. TOTAL's ann ret % divides by the portfolio's PEAK DEPLOYED capital - the peak of the summed series, which is lower than the sum of the per-strategy peaks unless they all peak together - and its avg P/L % pools per-trade percentages, each against its own strategy's capital.
Performance
Equity — cumulative P/L
Drawdown — the portfolio's
{# THE TWO KEYS. Both shades are the same grey at two densities, so each says plainly what it means and the overlap is left to read as what it is: neither condition holds there. #}
One point per close date; the drawdown keeps each day's WORST reading, so the trough here is the Max DD in the table rather than a shallower day-end version of it. Only the portfolio's drawdown is drawn — one curve per strategy on the same axes is a picture of nothing in particular.
Capital deployed
Each strategy's concurrent positions times its own capital, summed per SPX session. Half-open: a position is deployed on the sessions it is held through and not on the one it closes on, so this is capital still at risk at the close. The peak is what ann ret % divides by, and it is lower than the sum of the per-strategy peaks unless they all peak on the same day.
Monthly P/L
by year
Dated by close, like everything else here: a trade lands in the month it was closed in, whatever month it was opened in. Shade is the month's size against the biggest month in the grid.
P/L distribution
One bar per $100 of P/L, overlaid per strategy, on the filtered trades with qty applied.
Strategies active per day
Open positions per SPX session, with the portfolio total dotted. Same half-open count as Capital deployed — the old app counted this one inclusively and deduplicated same-day entries, which would make two charts on one page disagree about what a position is.
Rolling risk metrics
Sharpe Sortino Win rate % (right)
The old app's definitions, kept: P/L summed by close date, mean ÷ stdev × √252 for Sharpe and ÷ downside stdev for Sortino, win rate as the share of those DAYS that made money. The window counts days that had a close, not calendar days — at a couple of closes a week, 90 of them is closer to nine months than to three. Sortino is blank where a window holds fewer than two losing days: one loss is not a deviation.
Correlation
Metric vs P/L — Spearman, pooled
metricrhotrades
Strategy P/L — Pearson, weekly
pair
Metric vs P/L is rank correlation between each metric at ENTRY and the trade's P/L, over every strategy's filtered trades pooled — what the PORTFOLIO's P/L moved with, not what any one strategy's did. Nothing is reported from fewer than ten values. Strategy correlation is Pearson on weekly P/L: strategies close on their own schedules, so a daily series is mostly zeros and correlating mostly zeros measures how often two happened to close on the same day. Weeks where nothing closed anywhere are dropped for the same reason.
Rolling correlation
Every pair, over a window of weeks — the same basis as the matrix, so the two cannot disagree about what a correlation is. The pair chosen above is drawn in white.
Phase 6 of 7 — distribution, overlap, rolling risk
Strategies load, qty and capital allocate, the registry filters each one, the table totals them, and the curves draw: equity and drawdown together, capital deployed, and P/L by month. Every figure comes from the same code the single-backtest page uses, so a strategy reads the same on both — and the charts are built in the same pass as the table, from the same filtered trades, so they cannot disagree with the numbers above them.

A combination — the strategies, their qty, capital and filters, and the date-range mode — saves and loads by name.

Nothing to plot — the two axes must both be columns the table has resolved. Add them from the column chooser, or screen on them, which pulls them in.
Colour is TRIP COUNT, not profit. Profit splits the traded names into groups that do not correspond to what was learned — a name that made $20 across 6 trips and was abandoned because it was miserable to trade would sit beside one traded 226 times, and a name is pink for one bad trade out of eight. No ticker was ever abandoned for losing money. They were abandoned because bounciness, fillability and volume together made them untradeable — and that judgement was voted with attention. Trip count is that vote, and it is already in the data. These bands are a judgement, not a measurement. A name dropped after two minutes because it looked bouncy records an instinct rather than a measured property. It is still the best label available — but a metric that predicts these bands may be predicting the instinct, and that is a different claim from predicting tradeability. Scales. Log by default, linear automatically for a column whose units are a share — a 0–1 fraction on a log axis puts most of its range in the last tenth. Both are overridable per axis. On a log axis a zero cannot be drawn, so rows at exactly zero drop from that axis only. Constant-ratio lines appear only when both axes carry the same units and both are logarithmic. Spread bps over noise bps is a ratio; a share over a rate is not, and a diagonal drawn across that pair would imply a relationship that does not exist.

What this is for. A fetch once returned only Nasdaq's prints — missing NYSE, ARCA, BATS, EDGX and IEX — and the only symptom was trades/min reading 37% low. It took an hour to diagnose, and once the nightly job runs unattended it will happen again with nobody looking for it. One red row is the whole point. There is deliberately no distinct-exchange-code column: fetch.py already refuses any symbol-day under its minimum and does not write it, so a Nasdaq-only pull cannot reach compute. A count would measure a condition the guard prevents. What surfaced the problem was a RATE moving against its own history, so that is what this watches. Not every column can flag. unidentified-venue share sits near 0.0001, where a percentage change is arithmetic rather than information — 0.0001 to 0.0006 is +516%. Its level is still shown, because a jump to a few percent would mean the venue enum had gone stale; it just cannot raise an alarm. Any column whose baseline falls below the minimum is suppressed the same way. Each session is scored against the ones before it and never itself — a bad day inside its own baseline partly excuses itself, and at ten sessions one outlier moves the median it is being measured against.

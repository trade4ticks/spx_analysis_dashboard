What to trade today. The constraint this page exists for is finding tickers — performance varies about tenfold by name, and picking by eye each morning is what this replaces.

Filters read-time only — never triggers a recompute —
The pipeline computes and stores every symbol regardless of whether it passes. Moving a slider re-queries; rows for names that fail are still in the database, and they are the only data that can say whether a threshold is set in the right place.
Data health last ~10 sessions
phase 2 Per date: symbol count, median distinct exchange codes, median trades/min against trailing, fetch rejects. Exists because of a real failure: a fetch returned only Nasdaq's prints — missing NYSE, ARCA, BATS, EDGX and IEX — and the only symptom was trades/min reading 37% low. One red row is the whole point, and it matters most once the nightly job runs unattended and nobody is looking for it.
Ranked candidates one row per symbol passing the filters
phase 2 Ticker, price, spread ¢/bps, noise, ratio, quote coverage, trades/min, size, balance, off-exchange, 10-day ratio sparkline, traded marker. Quote coverage is a first-class column rather than a diagnostic: noise is measured between consecutive observed buckets, so a name at 0.33 coverage has observations ~30s apart at a nominal 10s horizon and its "10s noise" is really 30s noise. Coverage is the only thing that makes that comparable. Move-rate and move-bps sit beside the ratio, since noise conflates them and loses to the first term on sparse names.
Ticker detail session profile · repeatability · comparison · provenance
phase 3 When in the day this name is worth sitting in, and whether that window repeats. Session profile with actual trading hours shaded; a repeatability heatmap of sessions × 15-minute buckets; a comparison against names whose outcomes are already known; and provenance — what share of the tape the row was computed from, which condition codes were dropped, crossed quotes, records lost to same-timestamp collapsing.

"vs normal" compares this five minutes against the name's own 10-day median for the same clock time — against a daily average every name looks like it is deteriorating at noon, because every name is. Tape and book views are placeholders: a 15-minute delay is fine for "is this name in a good state" and useless for reading a tape.

Research

read at weekends

Everything below tests the tool rather than using it. spread ÷ noise is an untested hypothesis. It comes from one observation made while trading — that stocks moving faster than their spread are untradeable — and the entire pipeline is organised around it. If nothing separates, that is the finding.

The correlation matrix goes here in phase 4 — about 75 noise columns, most measuring nearly the same thing. If two variants correlate at 0.97 across the universe, one is redundant, and no trade data is needed to find that out.
Spread against noise
phase 4 Every candidate as a point, constant-ratio lines running diagonally. "Good" becomes a direction rather than a number, which is the geometry a ranked table hides — whether an unknown candidate sits in a known winner's neighbourhood or a known loser's. Traded names coloured by outcome.
Rank stability
phase 4 How far a ticker's rank moves day to day, per metric. No trade data needed. A metric whose top 20 reshuffles every session is measuring noise in the measurement rather than a property of the stock — one name read 0.069 bps then 1.727 with nothing changing, because the median statistic flipped across a boundary.
Calibration against realised results
phase 5 — needs the fills upload Rank correlation of each metric against actual $/min, sorted. With a handful of tickers and ~75 candidate metrics something will correlate by chance, so this is a table to watch over weeks rather than a verdict — the sample size shown prominently and the weakness made visible rather than implied.
Distributions
phase 4 Trade size through the session, gaps between trades, which side is consumed, best-bid lifetime. Round lots are price-tiered, so the odd-lot bands differ by name and their share rises midday — the middle of the day is not only quieter, the arrivals are smaller, and a 5-share print cannot fill a 50-share order.
Stability over time
phase 4 Noise across all sessions as small multiples on identical axes, and spread against noise on one axis. Separates level from stability. Where the two lines converge there is nothing to capture, however wide the spread looks in cents.
